Population vs Sample

  • A population is the entire set of individuals or items that we are interested in studying.

  • Population: “all subjects of interest”

  • Biological meaningful

  • A sample is a subset of the population that we actually observe or collect data from.

  • Sample: “a subset of the population”

Population vs Sample

If we collect data from the entire population, there is no need for inference.

Definitions

  • Population: The group towards which scientists attempt to generalize
  • Census: A study which includes all members of the population
  • Sample: A portion of the population which is part of the study
  • Study design: The process of obtaining the best sample to answer the research questions
  • Inference: The process of making generalizations about the population
  • Descriptive statistics: Methods for summarizing and/or presenting data
  • Inferential statistics: Methods for making generalizations about a population using information contained in the sample

Accuracy and precision

  • Accuracy: how close a measurement is to the true value
  • Precision: how close repeated measurements are to each other
  • Bias: systematic error that causes measurements to be consistently off from the true value
  • Variance: the degree to which repeated measurements vary from each other

Accuracy and Precision

Mean and variance

  • Mean: the average of a set of values
  • Variance: a measure of how much the values in a set differ from the mean
  • Standard deviation: the square root of the variance, which gives a measure of spread in the same units as the data

Central Limit Theorem

The central limit theorem states that the distribution of the sample mean will be approximately normally distributed, regardless of the shape of the population distribution, as long as the sample size is sufficiently large (usually n > 30).

Probability and Likelihood

  • What is probability? What is likelihood?

Probabiliy and Likelihood

  1. Probability: the chance of observing a particular outcome given a known model or distribution
  2. Likelihood: the plausibility of a model or estimate given observed data

Probability and inference

Statistical inference is founded in the ability to quantify uncertainty.

We use probability to quantify uncertainty in our estimates and predictions.

Probability tells us how likely a particular outcome is, given a known model or distribution.
